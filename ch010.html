PART ONE

Chapter 10修道院、教育与规训

背景

convent 不只是宗教场所，也是教育、慈善和纪律机构。理解称谓背后的等级，比记住全部宗教词更重要。

英文原文

10 A high fever took care of making amends, gripping me the instant I saw Mother Leonora shut me out as she went on praying, frozen as a corpse. A fever caused by fear, I think. How could I have been so foolish as to tell Mother Leonora what I was thinking? Shivering, my teeth chattering, I tried to make sense of what had happened to me. The terrible fever lasted three days and three nights, gouging my brain with the question: why did you do it? I had done it because like a fool, I who thought I was so clever had put too much faith in Mother Leonora. And so the disillusionment of seeing her cowardice day after day, and consequently, no longer loving her, had made me commit one of the most predictable errors. Once I realized my mistake, the fever went away. But not the terror of being exiled from all those women who, though fatuous and contemptible, were essential to me. For years they had seemed so sweet and beautiful and tall! They weren’t even tall. At fifteen I was already taller than Sister Costanza, who was the homeliest, true, but also the tallest. Too bad. I almost wished I could go back to the time when I admired them and tried to walk and talk like them. Careful, Modesta, even this desire to return to a past that can no longer be is a sentimental trap that can cost you dearly. No! Look at the reality: what happened happened and could not have been otherwise. Now I have to get out of this exile in which Mother Leonora has plunged me. In three days I haven’t seen anyone except the nursing sister and the old, bald doctor. If he were at least young! I wonder where Tuzzu is? Maybe he went to sea. The sea … now I knew what it was. I had glimpsed it in so many reproductions of famous paintings, they almost made me forget the desire I once had to see it. ‘What is the sea like, Tuzzu?’ ‘A vast stretch of water as far as the eye can see. See, it’s like this stony ground you see before you from morning till night. Only instead of these rocks and mud and – let’s not even mention the eyesores! – there’s water, blue water . Sometimes calm like the water in the well, sometimes tossing like the reeds when the fagoniu 7 blows.’ ‘So it’s just like in the paintings in the nuns’ house?’ ‘What are you saying, you silly ninny! Those pieces of canvas hung on the walls are fake, false and deceitful. Nature can’t be painted or bought. Besides, what can you expect from those shrivelled-up mummies? They’ve betrayed their own nature and all of nature, as my father and my uncle, God rest his soul, used to say. He could even read and write too. Barren, is what they are! They chose to be barren like the treacherous, deceitful sand. Never mind paintings! Come on, let’s walk a little, come …’ Tuzzu led me by the hand along an endless expanse of springy blue grass that swayed so much it made you feel like you do after drinking rosolio at Easter. ‘Such a silly monkey this picciridda is! First she pesters you like a fly because she wants to see the sea, then when you take her there she doesn’t even recognize it.’ The grass gave way beneath my feet and pulled me down; I clung to Tuzzu’s arm, terrorized … How did you save us from the fire, Tuzzu? ‘Don’t be afraid! Can’t you see I’m holding you? As long as I’m holding you, you don’t have to fear either water or fire.’ And it was so. I didn’t sink. And we walked along, hand in hand, in the blue sea of Tuzzu’s eyes. His hand was hot and gripped me tight … No, it wasn’t Tuzzu. It was that bald little man with the lizard eyes, gripping my wrist and shouting. He was always shouting, that little man. Maybe because he didn’t wear either a white or a black skirt? ‘Look, a seizure! And to make matters worse, her fever has risen again! This girl is going to die on us! Run, Sister Costanza, go immediately to Mother Leonora, and tell her that whatever sin this child has committed, she’d better come quickly or this one will die!’ So that was how to get out of there. That little man wasn’t as bad as he seemed. He must also be intelligent. I had to do as he said, and not be quiet and good like I had been those three days. I closed my eyes to rejoin Tuzzu and the sea that left me fearful and breathless. And with all the strength inspired by desire and terror, I cried out loudly, but with one small variation. Instead of Tuzzu’s name, I said: ‘Mother! Forgive me, Mother!’ And I thought of Tuzzu, whom I had forgotten for so long: ‘Forgive me, Mother, forgive me!’
10 一场高烧替我做了补救，一看到莱奥诺拉嬷嬷继续祈祷、像一具尸体般冰冷地把我拒之门外时，高烧就攫住了我。我想，这是恐惧引起的高烧。我怎么会蠢到告诉莱奥诺拉嬷嬷我心里在想什么？我瑟瑟发抖，牙齿打颤，试图理清发生在我身上的事。那可怕的高烧持续了三天三夜，一个问题掏空了我的脑子：你为什么那样做？我那样做是因为像个傻瓜一样，我这个自以为聪明的人，太相信莱奥诺拉嬷嬷了。于是，日复一日看到她的懦弱而幻想破灭，因而不再爱她，便让我犯了一个最可预见的错误。一旦意识到我的错误，烧就退了。但那种恐惧却没有退去——害怕被所有这些女人放逐，她们虽然虚浮可鄙，对我却至关重要。多年来她们看起来那么甜美、美丽、高大！她们甚至连高大都不是。十五岁时我已经比科斯坦扎修女高了，她是最丑的，没错，但也是最高的。太糟了。我几乎希望自己能回到过去，那个时候我还仰慕她们，试图像她们一样走路说话。小心，莫德斯塔，甚至连这种想要回到一个不再可能的过去的欲望，也是一个可能会让你付出沉重代价的感伤陷阱。不！直面现实：发生的已经发生，不可能不是这样。现在我得从莱奥诺拉嬷嬷把我抛入的这种放逐中挣脱出来。三天来，除了看护修女和那个秃头老医生，我没见过任何人。他要是年轻点也好啊！不知道图祖在哪里？也许他去了海上。 海……现在我知道它是什么了。我在那么多名画复制品中瞥见过它，几乎让我忘记了曾经想看它的渴望。 "海是什么样的，图祖？" "一片望不到边际的水域。你看，就像你从早到晚看到的这片石头地。只不过不是这些石头和泥浆——那些难看的东西更别提了！——而是水，蓝色的水。有时候静得像井水，有时候像法戈尼乌风吹拂时的芦苇一样翻腾。" "那就跟修女们房里的画一样咯？" "你说什么呢，你这傻丫头！那些挂在墙上的画布都是假的，虚假的、骗人的。大自然不能画，也买不到。再说，你能指望那些干瘪的木乃伊什么呢？她们背叛了自己的本性，背叛了整个大自然，正如我父亲和我叔叔——愿他安息——常说的。他还能读书写字呢。她们是贫瘠的！她们选择了像那阴险骗人的沙子一样贫瘠。别提什么画了！来吧，咱们走一走，来……" 图祖牵着我的手，沿着无边无际的蓝色弹力草地走，草地晃得让你感觉像复活节喝了玫瑰甜酒之后那样。 "这小丫头真是个傻猴子！先是像苍蝇一样缠着你要看海，等你带她去了，她反倒认不出来了。" 草在我脚下塌陷，把我往下拽；我抓住图祖的胳膊，吓坏了……你是怎么把我们从火里救出来的，图祖？ "别怕！你没看见我抓着你吗？只要我抓着你，你就不用怕水也不用怕火。" 确实如此。我没有沉下去。我们手牵手走在图祖眼睛那片蓝色的海里。他的手很热，紧紧握着我的手…… 不，不是图祖。是那个秃头小个子男人，长着一双蜥蜴眼睛，抓着我的手腕大声嚷嚷。他总是嚷嚷，那个小个子。也许是因为他既没穿白裙也没穿黑裙？ "看，发作了！更要命的是，她又烧起来了！这女孩要死在我们手里了！快跑，科斯坦扎修女，马上去找莱奥诺拉嬷嬷，告诉她不管这孩子犯了什么罪，她最好赶紧来，不然这孩子就要死了！" 原来这就是离开那里的办法。那个小个子并不像看上去那么坏。他一定也很聪明。我得照他说的做，不能像那三天那样安静乖巧。 我闭上眼睛，重回图祖和他那片让我既恐惧又窒息的海。怀着欲望和恐惧激发的全部力气，我大声呼喊，但做了一点小小的改动。没说图祖的名字，我说的是："嬷嬷！原谅我，嬷嬷！"我想起了图祖，那个我遗忘了那么久的人："原谅我，嬷嬷，原谅我！"
