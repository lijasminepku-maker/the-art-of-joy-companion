PART ONE

Chapter 17修道院、教育与规训

背景

convent 不只是宗教场所，也是教育、慈善和纪律机构。理解称谓背后的等级，比记住全部宗教词更重要。

英文原文

17 Starting that night, I got busy. I had to work quickly, because Mimmo was very good at convincing people, rich and poor, men and women, animals and devils, as Sister Teresa said. A saw was easy to find in the equipment shed behind the kitchen. There were saws of all shapes and sizes. And after gulping down the milk and soft, bland bread among all those flaccid white faces – I swear! what other colouring could all those future brides of Christ have? – instead of going to bed I waited until all the doors were closed before slipping out. Hugging the walls closely – I knew every stone of those corridors, every corner and every door – I climbed up toward the cool darkness, even darker than the pitch-black of the stairs. Luckily there was neither moon nor stars. Though the mornings were sunny, for days and days a dense cloud covering descended from sunset to dawn, obscuring Mother Leonora's firmament. She moaned about it – it wasn't the cloudy season – but for me it was a sign that I had to act, or saw, if you prefer. For several nights I sawed until dawn, protected by that mass of clouds until the first light of day. I sawed at four places, the four points that supported the weight of the telescope. When my work was done, I threw myself on my bed, exhausted – I hadn't slept for days and days – and contented. At last I could sleep. Now all I had to do was wait for a clear night. Strangely enough, however, I couldn't sleep a wink, maybe because I had become used to sleeping little or not at all, or because I was worried that the balustrade might be replaced. I would fall asleep but then quickly wake up, obsessed with keeping an eye on it. A clear night did not come. It rained now, even in the daytime. 'What a shame, princess, just this year when nature promised such a great harvest! There hasn't been such a spell of foul weather around here as long as anyone can remember. All that good wheat and hay will be ruined if this continues.' I prayed along with Mimmo that the skies would clear, because otherwise my wheat and hay would rot as well if things went on like this. There was nothing I could do. At night, clinging to the window bars, I almost wept with rage. Not a star could be seen, not a breath of wind stirred that dark, dense mass. Exhausted, I sank onto the bed. Let it all rot, wheat and rye and hay. That night I would sleep. I'd had enough. And I slept so deeply that, according to what they told me afterwards, only Sister Costanza's slaps – she never missed an opportunity – were able to wake me up. Howling, weeping, doors slamming in the wake of the frantic clanging of the bell, dragged me out of bed terrified. I thought: an earthquake! 'Worse than that, child! Worse! Hurry, come to the chapel, you're the only one missing. We're all in the chapel, praying. Mother Leonora fell from the tower! Who would have expected it?' I had never heard such joy in Sister Costanza's sorrowful voice. 'Who would have thought she'd go up to her observatory! There's been nothing but thunder and lightning all night. Who would have imagined it! Come, hurry! Mimmo has laid her out as best he could; he's the one who heard the scream. Come to the chapel to see her one last time and keep vigil for her!' Keep vigil? Me? All night and maybe the next morning too, when I was dying for some sleep? I wouldn't dream of it. 'Come, child, get a move on! Don't stand there in a daze. Naturally, I understand how you feel; you are the one most affected by this tragedy. You were so devoted to her and she loved you so much! But take heart. Accept this great trial that God has sent you.' So then, if I were the most affected, I could very well faint out of grief and thus avoid the ordeal they wanted to inflict on me. E caddi come corpo morto cade , I fell like a dead body falls, as the Poet and master of life says. And there was no way to wake me, either that night or the following day.
17 自那夜起，我便忙活起来。我必须迅速行动，因为米莫极善于说服人——无论贫富、男女、禽兽还是魔鬼——如特蕾莎修女所言。 锯子在厨房后面的工具棚里极易找到。那里有各种形状大小的锯子。在那些苍白松弛的面孔之间——我发誓，那些基督的未来新娘还能有什么别的面色？——吞下牛奶和软绵绵淡而无味的面包后，我没有上床，而是等到所有门都关闭后才溜出去。紧贴着墙壁——我熟悉那些走廊的每一块石头、每一个角落、每一扇门——我爬上清凉的黑暗，比楼梯的漆黑还要暗。所幸没有月亮，也没有星星。尽管清晨阳光明媚，但一连数日，从日落到黎明，浓密的云层低垂，遮住了莱奥诺拉院长的苍穹。她为此哀叹——这并非多云季节——但于我而言，这是一个必须行动的征兆，或者如果你愿意，可称之为锯的征兆。连续数夜，我锯到黎明，受那团云层庇护，直至第一缕晨光。我在四个位置下锯——支撑望远镜重量的四个支点。活计完成后，我倒在床上，筋疲力尽——我已数日数夜未眠——然而心满意足。我终于可以睡了。如今只需等待一个晴朗之夜。 然而奇怪的是，我竟无法合眼，也许是因为我已习惯睡得极少或根本不睡，也许是因为我担心那栏杆被更换。我会睡着，但旋即惊醒，一心惦记着盯住它。晴朗之夜没有到来。如今连白日也在下雨。 "真可惜，公主，偏偏今年大自然许诺了如此丰饶的收成！此地还从未有过这般漫长的恶劣天气，据人们记忆所及。若这样持续下去，所有那些好麦子和干草都要毁了。"我与米莫一同祈祷天空放晴，否则若这般继续下去，我的麦子和干草也会烂掉。 我无计可施。夜里，抓着窗栏，我几乎愤恨得落泪。不见一颗星星，没有一丝风吹动那黑暗浓密的云层。筋疲力尽，我倒在床上。让一切烂掉吧，小麦、黑麦、干草。那夜我要睡觉。我受够了。而我睡得如此深沉，据后来她们告诉我，唯有科斯坦扎修女的耳光——她从不放过任何机会——才将我唤醒。嚎叫、哭泣、门在疯狂钟声的催促下砰砰作响，将我从床上惊恐地拖起。我想：地震了！ "比那更糟，孩子！更糟！快来，到小堂去，只缺你一人了。我们都在小堂里祈祷。莱奥诺拉院长从塔上摔下来了！谁能料到呢？" 我从未在科斯坦扎修女那悲哀的声音中听见过如此欢欣。 "谁会想到她竟上了天文台！整夜只有雷鸣电闪。谁能想象得到！来，快！米莫已尽其所能将她安顿好；是他听到了尖叫。来小堂见她最后一面，为她守夜吧！" 守夜？我？一整夜，也许还要加上第二天早晨，当我渴睡得要死的时候？我做梦也不会干。 "来，孩子，快点！别站在那儿发愣。自然，我明白你的感受；你是受这场悲剧影响最深的人。你曾那般忠心待她，她也如此深爱于你！但振作起来吧。接受上帝赐予你的这场大试炼。" 那么，既然我是受影响最深之人，我大可因悲伤而晕厥，从而避开她们想强加于我的煎熬。我如死尸般倒下——正如那位诗人和生命之主所言。而那夜及次日，再无方法能将我唤醒。
